AN ORIGINAL FICTIONAL UNIVERSE

The Chronicle
of the Impossible.

Paris, 1789. A goldsmith begins a work that no artisan can finish.

In a city on the edge of revolution, a workshop stood behind a door that appeared on no official map. Its proprietor was known only as Le Fol Orfèvre—the Mad Goldsmith.

He believed that gold was not precious merely because it was rare, but because human beings gave it meaning. He refused commissions made only to display wealth. He preferred the inventor whose work had been rejected, the cartographer who questioned every map, the patron who funded ideas without demanding credit.

Les Sept Folies

He proposed seven creations: Audace, Volonté, Temps, Mémoire, Invention, Intégrité and Éternité. The first six would take physical form. The seventh would not.

Six can be crafted. One must be earned.

As the city fractured, the six objects were separated. The archive disappeared. The members of the circle became rumours. The goldsmith was never seen again.

The seventh absence

Centuries later, the six creations are still the subject of conflicting accounts. Some believe they are keys to a hidden mechanism. Others believe they were never intended to be reunited.

The final manuscript offers a different explanation: the seventh creation cannot be made from gold, because gold can be bought. It cannot be made by an artisan, because an artisan can only give it form.

It is the sum of what a person dares, protects, invents, remembers and leaves behind.

Do not make the seventh. Become worthy of it.
